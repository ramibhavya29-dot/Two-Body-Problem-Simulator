A QUICK FIELD GUIDE

Reading the orbit

↗
01 / MOTION

Gravity shapes the path

Newton's inverse-square law pulls both masses together. As each body accelerates, its velocity continually changes direction, producing an orbit rather than a straight line. With zero total momentum the center of mass stays fixed; otherwise it moves at constant velocity.

02 / TRAJECTORY

Bound or unbound?

Negative relative orbital energy means a bound ellipse (a circle is a special case). Zero or positive relative energy gives a parabolic or hyperbolic escape path. Eccentricity e classifies the conic: e < 1, = 1, or > 1.

03 / KEPLER

Three laws, in motion

Orbits are conic sections with the center of mass at a focus. Equal areas are swept in equal times because angular momentum is conserved. The period follows T² ∝ a³, with both masses included in this two-body model.

NUMERICAL NOTE

Stable steps, safe contact

Velocity Verlet advances both bodies with a fixed SI time step. There is no force softening; contact is detected using estimated spherical radii (Earth-like density for custom masses), then the simulation pauses before the inverse-square force becomes singular.
